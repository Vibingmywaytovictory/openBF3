## 5. Navigation

### 5.1 The input set equals the motor's collision set

The motor collides with `query_mask::kMovement = Collision | Character` (static_world.h, `CharacterVirtual`'s
object-layer filter; m1.md §5.2). A soup triangle's layer is `StaticLayer(body)`. S1 makes that one function,
`static_layer_of` (static_world.h), and both the static world's tile cook and `nav_tile_inputs` call it, so the two
sets cannot drift. The nav input of a tile is the triangles of `static_layer_of(t.body) ∈ kMovement` from the same
`CollisionSoup` (same `SoupOptions`, terrain cover included) as the static world. Concretely it contains every Collision
body (static model groups, RBTypeCollision bodies of meshes, invisible blockers, the terrain cells with holes left open)
and every Character body (PLAYERCLIP); it never contains a Raycast body (railings, glass and foliage shells, see-through
detail), a part only a later health state shows (the soup drops those), or an object the level starts hidden (listed,
not built). Foliage and scatter enter only when their Havok body is Collision, and then the motor collides with them
too.

`sim.nav` proves the equality on Metro CQL and Caspian CQL, three ways:

1. **Per tile, by digest.** For every soup tile, the SHA-256 over its own movement-layer triangles (float bits, layer,
   in soup order), as `nav_tile_inputs` hashes its first `own_triangles`, equals the same digest computed over the
   static world's cook input, and the count equals `StaticTileInfo::triangles[Collision] + triangles[Character]`.
2. **No foreign layer.** The input holds 0 Raycast triangles; the report lists the movement-set triangles by surface
   class (foliage and glass counts included), so what the motor collides with is visible.
3. **By geometry.** 10,000 seeded rays (vertical drops and horizontal eye-height rays inside the region): the first hit
   of `CollisionWorld::ray(…, kMovement)` equals a brute-force double-precision ray test against the nav input
   triangles of the tiles the ray crosses, within 1 mm (ambiguous grazing hits counted, not judged, as in `sim.world`).

### 5.2 Terrain cover: the motor needs the ground the nav walks on

The default soup keeps terrain cells only in tiles holding Havok triangles and their 8 neighbours (collision_soup.h).
That leaves combat-area ground without collision: 35 of Caspian CQL's 273 combat-area tiles and 1,119 of Kharg's
1,494 hold no soup triangle at all (§4). A walker sent there would fall through the world, and a nav baked "over the
combat areas" from that soup would have holes the bots route around. So S1 adds `SoupOptions::terrain_cover`: extra
tiles whose terrain cells are kept as well. S1 passes the level's combat-area tiles (§5.3) to both the static world
(`StaticWorldLoadOptions::terrain_cover`, `SharedLoadOptions::terrain_cover`) and the nav, so the motor and the nav see
the same ground.

* An empty cover gives the M1 soup bit for bit. The level-oracle parity (230 runs) and M1's tests and cache entries are
  untouched, and `kCollisionSoupVersion` stays 1.
* A non-empty cover adds `cover=<SHA-256 of the sorted tile list>` to the "jolt-world" key. Tiles are content keyed
  anyway; most tiles hash identically with and without the cover, so they are shared.
* **Deep water [inferred, to measure].** Kharg's cover reaches the sea bottom. S1 has no swimming (the motor's
  Swimming is a stub, m1.md §6.9), so walkers on the sea bed would be wrong but harmless. The cover takes a combat-area
  tile only when at least one of its terrain samples lies above the water plane − 1.8 m (one soldier height). The plane
  is the WaterEntityData's transform y (0 on Kharg). If that height proves wrong, the cover keeps every tile and the
  sea bed stays walkable for S1 (G4 timing is then measured on the larger set; reported).

### 5.3 Bake

* **Region.** `level_combat_tiles` traverses every MP mode of the level, reads its combat areas (`read_mode_areas`) and
  takes the union of the 64 m tiles their polygons, grown by `region_margin` 32 m (gap load §2.7), touch (plan §3.6:
  "bake per level over the union of its modes' combat areas"). Every mode of the level then bakes the same tiles, and a
  tile whose input is the same in two modes is one cache entry.
* **Tiles.** The soup's 64 m grid; tile (x, z) covers [64x, 64x + 64) × [64z, 64z + 64). Each tile rasterises the
  triangles of the 3 × 3 soup tiles around it that reach into its border: walkable radius + 3 cells, as Recast's
  Sample_TileMesh does. Tiles therefore bake independently and in parallel, and their borders match at the seams.
  The heightfield's y range is the input's, capped at `kMaxNavTileHeight` (§9).
* **Parameters** [plan §3.6; soldier verified §4]: cs 0.25, ch 0.1; walkable climb 0.3 m = 3 cells, so climb / ch is
  whole (topics/06 §2.4: ch 0.3 broke Metro's stairs); slope 50°; radius 0.35 m.
  * **Radius rounding.** At cs 0.25 the radius is 2 cells = 0.5 m with Recast's `ceil` (`radius_round_up`, the default
    and what gap load §2.7 measured), or 1 cell with round-to-nearest. 0.5 m erodes 0.15 m more than the capsule on each
    side, so a 0.9 m doorway that the capsule fits (0.7 m) closes in the nav. The gate runs with `ceil`. When §5.7
    finds a split at a passage between 0.7 and 1.0 m wide, the spike re-runs G2 with 1 cell and reports both. 1 cell
    is acceptable because the corridor's corners stay at least 0.25 m off walls and the motor slides along the rest
    (m1.md §6.1).
  * **Height.** The walkable height is the crouch height, 1.15 m = 12 cells. After `rcBuildCompactHeightfield`, every
    span with less than the stand height of room (1.8 m = 18 cells) gets area `kNavAreaCrouch`, so crouch-only
    passages are in the mesh and the brain crouches through them. Prone-only space (< 1.15 m) is not walkable.
  * **Recast settings.** Watershed partitioning (`NavPartition::Watershed`, the Recast sample's default and gap load
    §2.7's); max edge 12 m; simplification error 1.3; min / merge region 8² / 20² cells; detail sample distance 6
    cells, error 1 ch; 6 verts per poly (Detour's maximum).
* **Drop links** (topics/06 §2.6 item 1). Each border edge of a tile's poly mesh (an edge without a neighbour) is
  sampled every 1 m. A landing within `drop_reach` 1.0 m outward and at most `drop_max` 4.9 m lower (IW4L's 192 in; a
  4.9 m fall lands at 9.8 m/s, below the 13.9 m/s damage knee) becomes a one-way off-mesh link (`kNavAreaDrop`,
  `nav_flags::kDrop`). The landing is found against the tile's own input, border included, so links are tile-local and
  content-keyed. A stand-capsule sweep on the CollisionWorld (`link_check`) must find the drop path clear. Jump links
  (gaps, low walls up to the 0.6 m jump) and ladders are not built in S1 (§13).
* **64-bit poly refs** (`RECASTNAVIGATION_DT_POLYREF64 ON`, top-level CMakeLists S1 line). With 32-bit refs a
  4,096-tile mesh leaves 10 bits for polys per tile (1,024), and Kharg's CQL alone touches 1,494 combat-area tiles.
  Dense 64 m tiles of Metro's station can exceed 1,024 polys [inferred].
* **Assembly.** `dtNavMesh::init` (tile size 64 m, `maxTiles` = next power of two of the baked tiles, origin (0, 0, 0)
  so Detour's tile coordinates are the soup's), then `addTile` in (x, z) order.
* **Cost** [plan §3.6, gap load §2.7: BF3 capsule on the level_bf3 soup, 16 threads]: Caspian 5.18 s, Kharg 11.84 s,
  Metro TDM 0.17 s; tiles of terrain only are cheap. The gates (G4) are those numbers plus margin. Our regions differ
  from the research's (combat union over all modes, the terrain cover, water rule), so G4 prints the tile counts
  (terrain-only and object tiles) next to the times.

### 5.4 Cache

Through the existing `Cache` and `cached_convert` (m1.md §5.5 conventions; engine = `cache_engine_id()`, converter
`kNavConverterVersion`, format `kNavFormatVersion`, sources empty: the digests stand for them).

| kind | key params | payload |
|---|---|---|
| `nav-tile` | `input=<NavTileInput::digest hex>;` + `nav_params_text` + `;` + `recast_build_id()` (`recast=1.6.0;polyref64=1;verts_per_poly=6`); `jolt_version = jolt_build_id()` when drop links were checked on Jolt | "OBF3NTIL" v1: NavTileInfo, then the Detour tile data (`dtCreateNavMeshData` output) |
| `nav-world` | `source=<static world source digest>;level=<lower>;mode=<lower>;soup=<kCollisionSoupVersion>;terrain_spacing=<f32 bits>;cover=<SHA-256 of the cover tiles>;region=<SHA-256 of the region tiles>;` + `nav_params_text` + recast id; same versions | "OBF3NWLD" v1: per tile x, z, input digest, NavTileInfo |
| `nav-area` | `level=<lower>;margin=<f32 bits>;modes=<mode list>;sources=<SHA-256 over every mode's source digest>` | "OBF3NARA" v1: the region tiles and, per mode, its objectives and combat polygons |

* **Warm path.** The static world's source digest → `nav-world` → every `nav-tile` → `validate_detour_tile` →
  `addTile`. No soup, no Recast.
* **Cold path.** The soup (the static world's cold path already built it; else `load_nav_world_cached` builds it) →
  `nav_tile_inputs` → per tile a hit by input digest or a bake and put → the world entry last, only when every tile was
  stored (as `jolt-world`).
* `nav-area` needs every mode's source digest, so a warm read still mounts each mode (≈ 0.2 s each, reported). An
  unavailable digest only costs the shortcut.

### 5.5 Connectivity (G2)

* **Graph.** The polys of every tile, linked by Detour's links: internal neighbours and tile portals, which are both
  ways, and off-mesh drop links, which are one way (start → link → landing). `nav_components` computes the **strongly
  connected components** (iterative Tarjan, O(polys + links)). A bot must be able to reach every objective from every
  spawn and come back, which one weakly connected piece with one-way drops does not guarantee. The weak count is
  printed as well. The main component is the largest by walkable area.
* **Rows.** One per flag, HQ, MCOM (mode_areas.h) and AlternateSpawn point (spawn_points.h) of the mode, enabled or
  not (the mode's logic enables them during a round).
  * A spawn is checked at its **settled feet**: `settle_spawn` on the S1 static world, the position the server
    spawns at. If the settle fails, the raw point is used and the row says so.
  * `nav_check_point` takes the nearest poly in a box around the point.
  * A row is **on the mesh** when the horizontal offset is at most the kind's radius:
    * spawn: 1.0 m, |vertical| ≤ 1.0 m. The settle shifts up to 0.5 m, and the 0.5 m erosion of §5.3 can put a
      spawn 0.35 m from a wall just off the mesh;
    * MCOM: 2.0 m. The arm radius is 1.5 m [verified], plus the erosion;
    * flag and HQ: 5.0 m [inferred]. A capture point's transform is the flag pole, which has collision of its own;
      a soldier only has to stand in the zone.
* **Gate.** Every row of every mode on the mesh and in the main component. The row prints kind, index, position,
  horizontal and vertical offset, its component's id, poly count and area.

### 5.6 Queries

`NavQuery` (nav_query.h): one dtNavMeshQuery with a 4,096-node pool per instance (topics/06 §2.4's harness used a
65,535-node pool for full-map queries; the bots' sliced searches need far fewer per step, and a search that runs out
returns a partial path that the brain repaths from). It offers nearest poly, random point inside a component, and
sliced A* (begin / continue with an iteration budget / finish, at most 512 polys). `NavCorridor` wraps dtPathCorridor:
`move_position` follows the agent along the surface and reports when it left the corridor, and `corners` gives up to
8 straight-path corners with the area and flags of the poly each corner leaves into.

### 5.7 The Metro split: how S1 finds and fixes it

Research: with the BF3 capsule the largest component holds 43.4 % (IW4L soup, topics/06 C11) and 23 % (level_bf3
soup, gap load §2.7). The objects at the split points are SOLID 2–4.6 m tall shapes standing on the floor, at (−171.5,
−258.9), (−131.7, −235.3) and (−150.2, −273.2), and a 2.4 m SOLID + GLASS object at (−172, 59.3, −259) that the human
trace walks through. The cause is open. The prime suspect is IW4L treating Raycast bodies as SOLID (handoff 04 §3.2).
The procedure:

1. **Bake twice:** with `layer_mask = kMovement` (the gate's input) and with `kAll` (Raycast included, the research's
   situation). `bf3dump nav components MP_Subway ConquestLarge0 --layers=movement|all` prints the components (polys,
   area, bounds) and, for the two largest, their **seams**: pairs of polys of different components within 1.0 m
   horizontally and 0.5 m vertically. Those are the split points.
2. **Name the objects at each seam:** `bf3dump nav probe <x> <y> <z> <radius>` lists the soup placements with
   triangles within the radius of the point: placement kind, entity, res, body kind, surface, flags, entity type and
   triangle count.
3. **Decide by the data, one rule per object class, the same for the motor and the nav:**
   * a Raycast body: already out of both sets (§5.1);
   * an object BF3 soldiers pass but our static world builds solid (a gate or door that starts open, a prop that is a
     dynamic body in BF3, a part a later state shows): it leaves **both** sets through a soup classification, a
     tag flag plus `static_layer_of`, never through the nav alone, so §5.1's equality holds. The evidence (entity
     type, Physics data, name) is written in §15;
   * a real obstacle our soldier also cannot pass (a kiosk, a counter): the split is real for our sim, and the gate
     needs a link (a jump link over a ≤ 0.6 m obstacle) or the point is reported as unreachable with its object.
4. The result table (split points, objects, decision, component share before and after) goes into this section's
   "As built".

## 6. Server scale

### 6.1 What one tick contains, and how it is measured

A GameServer tick (game_server.h order, M1 §8.1), as `run_scale_bench` times it with `steady_clock` around each part
(`GameServerStats` S1 fields):

| part | content | field |
|---|---|---|
| commands | per slot, the command for this tick from the jitter buffer, else the repeat (the observer's hold command) | `last_commands_ms` |
| command sources | `PathService::run` (sliced A* steps, ≤ `iterations_per_tick` across all bots), then every bot's `WalkerBrain::command` in slot order: per tick a cheap steering step (no nav query); on the bot's staggered think tick one `NavCorridor::move_position`, the corners, arrival / stuck checks, and on a new goal a `random_point` or nearest-poly query and a path request | `last_sources_ms` (`bots.last_brains_ms`, `last_paths_ms`) |
| motor | one `SoldierMotor::step` per live soldier (65): CharacterVirtual reset + `RefreshContacts` + `ExtendedUpdate` (sweeps, stair walk) + the motor's ground hug and pose-fit queries (m1.md §6.1) | `last_step_ms` |
| rules | respawns (each a `settle_spawn`: 60 motor steps), dev requests (none in the bench) | `last_rules_ms` |
| snapshots | every second tick, one snapshot per observer: own state + up to ≈ 55 others nearest first (the 1,200 B cap, m1.md §7.3) | `last_encode_ms` |
| whole | `GameServer::tick` | `last_tick_ms` |

* **Back to back.** The bench calls `GameServer::tick` back to back on one thread: no sleeps, no ENet service (the
  datagram path's cost is ENet's, outside GameServer). So a tick's wall time is its single-thread CPU time, the
  quantity a 60 Hz ServerHost thread must fit into 16.7 ms and the gate into 8 ms.
* **Spawns.** The initial spawns of 64 bots (64 settles on the first ticks) fall in the 10 s warm-up and are reported
  separately (`spawn_tick_ms`). A respawn inside the window (a fall death; drops are ≤ 4.9 m, so none is expected)
  stays in the percentiles and is counted.
* **Mode.** ConquestLarge0 on both levels: the 64-player mode, with 73 (Metro) and 155 (Caspian) spawn points, so 64
  spawns find points (TDM Caspian has 65). Bots alternate teams 1 and 2.
* **Encode scaling.** The bench also runs (printed, not gated) with `observers = 16`, to show what encoding for more
  peers costs. M3's gate (64 connections, tick < 8 ms) is M3's.

### 6.2 Budget expectations [plan, inferred]

Plan §3.5: server tick 4–6 ms of 16.7 [inferred]; topics/06 §3.9 / C17: bots ≈ 0.3–0.9 ms per tick single-threaded,
dominated by perception rays, which walkers do not cast; motor ≈ 64 × 1–2 µs per tick [inferred] against M1's
measured ≈ 2 µs per step on a busy synthetic course and real Metro geometry unmeasured. So G1 is expected to pass
with margin; the spike's value is the measured split.

### 6.3 Sweep cost (G3)

* `CollisionWorld::query_count()` counts the world's narrow-phase queries. There is one broadphase layer, and Jolt's
  BroadPhaseQuadTree calls `ShouldCollide(BroadPhaseLayer)` exactly once per ray, cast or collide query [verified:
  BroadPhaseQuadTree.cpp, every query loops the layers once]. So a counting filter in `CollisionWorld::Impl`'s per-mask
  broadphase filters counts every CharacterVirtual sweep, contact collect, stair probe and the motor's own queries.
* The report: motor steps, queries per step, queries per second of sim time at 64 walkers (plan R1's estimate:
  64 × 5 × 60 ≈ 19 k/s), µs per step (`step_ms` / soldiers), and µs per query (step time / queries, an upper bound,
  since the solver's own math is included).

### 6.4 Detour costs and nav memory

* From the server NavQuery's counters (`NavQueryStats`): calls and mean µs of sliced paths (init + updates +
  finalize), corridor moves and nearest-poly queries, per second of sim time.
* `bf3dump nav path` runs 1,000 seeded full A* queries between random points of the main component and prints mean /
  p95 / max ms and path lengths (research: Metro 0.10–0.11 ms mean, Caspian 0.28 / 1.69 ms, Kharg 0.92 / 4.48 ms).
* Memory: `NavTileInfo::data_bytes` per tile (mean, max) and in total, plus the peak working set of the bake.

## 7. Walker brain (bots/walker_brain.h)

* **Contract.** A brain reads a `WalkerView`: its own authoritative state after tick − 1, the tick, the goal set,
  and the components and NavQuery / PathService of the server thread. It returns the `Command` for the tick, the same
  struct a human's client sends. It never writes sim state, never teleports, never reads another soldier. That is
  plan §3.6 "reads an immutable view, writes Commands only" and D11.
* **Goals.** Every goal is in the main strongly connected component, so every goal is reachable from every bot on it.
  * `objective_share` of the goals are mode objectives: flags, HQs and MCOMs, plus spawn points so TDM / SQDM have
    objectives.
  * The rest are `random_point`s at least 20 m away.
  * Arrival: within 1.5 m horizontally and 1.5 m vertically.
* **Path.** A `PathService` request; the answer, Done or Partial, becomes a `NavCorridor`. A Partial or Failed answer
  counts and is repathed from the corridor's end, or the goal is dropped after `max_repaths`.
* **Steering, every tick** (no nav query):
  * the target is the next corner, or the one after it once within `corner_lookahead`;
  * yaw: turned towards the target at most 540°/s, i.e. 9° per tick (research topics/06 §1: ≈ 550°/s);
  * move axes: the remaining direction in the yaw frame, `quantize_axis(cos)` forward and `quantize_axis(sin)` right,
    so a bot walks while it turns;
  * pose: `Crouch` when the current or next poly is `kNavAreaCrouch`, or during a crouch leg (`crouch_share` of the
    legs), else `Stand`;
  * sprint: on a sprint leg (`sprint_share`), when standing, on walk area, and the next corner is ≥ 6 m straight ahead
    within 20°. Never sprint while crouched, which would stand the soldier up (`sprint_stands_up`).
* **Think**, staggered: period = round(60 / think_hz) = 6 ticks at 10 Hz, phase = slot % period, so about 11 of the 64
  bots think per tick. A think runs `move_position` to the bot's position (repath when it left the corridor), refreshes
  the corners, and checks arrival, progress and stuck.
* **Stuck.** Movement commanded but less than 0.5 m of progress over 2 s (IW4L `STUCK_TICKS` 40 at 20 Hz) → repath
  from the current position; after 3 in a row, a new goal (counted as abandoned). A bot with no poly within 2 m walks
  back to the nearest poly (`off_mesh_ticks`). All counted in `WalkerStats`; G1's validity condition reads them.
* **Determinism.** Per-bot splitmix64 seeded from the match seed and the slot, brains in slot order, PathService first
  come first served: a bench run reproduces (host.scale.unit checks a hash over every command).

## 8. GameServer integration (bot_fill.h)

* **Config.** `GameServerConfig::bots` (additive). `GameServer::init` adds `count` server-side soldiers with
  `add_local_soldier`, teams 1 and 2 alternating, each a `CommandSource` forwarding to its WalkerBrain. That is the
  path the M1 scripted walkers take, so the motor, spawns, snapshots and stats are unchanged.
* **Director.** One per GameServer (bot_fill.cpp) owns the server thread's NavQuery and PathService. The first
  `CommandSource::command` call of a tick runs `PathService::run`, so GameServer's tick needs no new step.
* **Stats.** GameServer copies the director's `BotFillStats` and its tick-part times into `GameServerStats`
  (additive fields; ServerHost publishes them unchanged).
* **Loading.** `load_bot_level` runs on the loader after `load_shared_level`, which must have used the same terrain
  cover; it checks the cover digest and fails otherwise.
* **Slots.** `max_slots` must hold the bots, the walkers and the peers: the bench uses 128, the game with `--bots`
  128.

## 9. Hostile-input bounds

Install data and cache blobs are hostile. Every rule fails with an error, never a crash, hang or unbounded allocation.

* **mode_areas** (bf3_core): every array, connection and name it scans or compares charges the World's `WorkBudget`
  before it is used (the M0 rule). At most 256 combat areas of 4,096 points each.
* **nav_tile_inputs**: at most 2^21 input triangles per tile; non-finite corners are already dropped by the soup.
* **Recast, per tile:**
  * before rasterising, the sum over its triangles of the cells their XZ bounding boxes cover is charged against
    2^28. A soup of huge overlapping triangles fails the tile instead of allocating spans without end. The rest of
    Recast's pipeline (compact heightfield, distance field, regions, contours, poly mesh, detail) is linear in the
    spans or bounded by the region count;
  * the y range is capped at 800 m (13-bit span heights at ch 0.1);
  * a Recast step that fails names the tile;
  * at most 2^16 tiles per world.
* **Detour tile data from the cache.** `dtNavMesh::addTile` checks only the magic and the version. So
  `validate_detour_tile` recomputes Detour 1.6.0's layout from the header counts (the aligned sections of
  `dtCreateNavMeshData`) against the data size, then checks every index before Detour sees it:
  * poly vertex indices < vertCount; neis 0, an external-link flag, or ≤ polyCount; vertCount per poly ≤ 6;
  * detail meshes inside the detail arrays; BV nodes inside the tree;
  * off-mesh connection polys inside the tile; the tile coordinates equal the key's tile;
  * finite coordinates inside the header bounds.

  It mirrors Detour's private constants; a Recast bump changes `recast_build_id()` and with it every key. The
  `sim.nav.unit` fuzz mutates 2,000 real tile blobs; each is refused, or added and queried without fault (also under
  asan).
* **Blobs.** Every count is checked against the bytes left before anything is allocated; trailing bytes are refused.
* **Queries.** Node pools are fixed per NavQuery, paths ≤ 512 polys, corners ≤ 8; sliced searches run within their
  iteration budget; the brain clamps its axes and yaw to the Command's ranges.

## 14. Risks and the fallback rule

| risk | where it shows | response |
|---|---|---|
| R1: tick over budget | G1 p99 > 8 ms | the rule below |
| R6: the nav does not connect (Metro split) | G2 rows outside the main component | §5.7: name the seam objects, fix the motor and nav set together by evidence, or add jump links; else report unreachable points with their objects |
| R18: bakes too slow | G4 over threshold | Monotone partitioning (no distance field; faster, longer polys), no detail mesh on terrain-only tiles (the heightfield is already its detail), per-class timing printed to see which tiles cost; cs stays 0.25 (plan) unless the user decides otherwise |
| the terrain cover enlarges the static world | the S1 world's cold load and memory (printed) | content-keyed tiles; the cover is opt-in (M1 unchanged) |
| Kharg's sea bed | G4 tile count, nav polys under water | §5.2's water rule, reported |
| radius rounding closes passages | §5.7 seams at 0.7–1.0 m passages | the 1-cell variant, reported beside |
| POLYREF64 is a shared build option | other tracks' Detour use (none today: nav_link.cpp only allocates) | one CMake line, documented |
| combat-area links resolved by field id | an unusual mode wiring | `read_mode_areas` follows LinkConnections generically (trigger → interface → SubWorld link → VolumeVectorShape) and fails loudly when a trigger resolves to no shape; per-mode counts printed |

**Fallback rule (plan: 30 Hz sim, fewer sweeps, own BVH behind ICollisionWorld).** If G1 fails on a level, the
bench's split decides, in this order:

1. **Motor ≥ 60 % of the p99 tick** (`step_ms`):
   * **queries per step > 8**: the solver casts too often. Try *fewer sweeps* first: `max_collision_iterations` 5 → 3
     and the walk-stairs pass only when the first move was blocked (motor tuning keys, no interface change). Rerun
     `host.scale`. If p99 ≤ 8 ms and `sim.motor` still passes (E1 unchanged), that is the recommendation;
   * **µs per query > 3** with ≤ 8 queries per step: the queries themselves are slow on our MeshShapes. That is the
     *own BVH* case (behind the CollisionWorld queries, m1.md §5.6). It is not built in S1. The design doc records
     the measured µs per query and the share of the tick it would save;
   * otherwise: *30 Hz*. Rebuild with `kSimHz = 30` (one constant, m1.md §6.11) and rerun the same bench. The gate
     reading is then "p99 ≤ 8 ms per 30 Hz tick", half the per-second work at the same per-tick budget. The motor's
     30 Hz E1 behaviour is S5 harness H:T1's question, not S1's.
2. **Sources (brains + nav) ≥ 30 %**: lower `think_hz` to 5 and `iterations_per_tick` to 2,048; then brains on a
   job pool (one NavQuery per worker).
3. **Encode ≥ 30 %**: priority-limited encoding (M3's ghost priority).

The doc's §6 "As built" gets the table of measured shares, the variant tried and its p99, and the recommendation.
