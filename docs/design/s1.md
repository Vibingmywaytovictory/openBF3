## 5. Navigation

### 5.1 The input set equals the motor's collision set

The motor collides with `query_mask::kMovement = Collision | Character` (static_world.h, `CharacterVirtual`'s
object-layer filter; m1.md §5.2). A soup triangle's layer is `StaticLayer(body)`. S1 makes that one function,
`static_layer_of` (static_world.h), and both the static world's tile cook and `nav_tile_inputs` call it, so the two
sets cannot drift. The nav input of a tile is the triangles of `static_layer_of(t.body) ∈ kMovement` from the same
`CollisionSoup` (same `SoupOptions`, terrain cover included) as the static world. Concretely it contains every Collision
body (static model groups, RBTypeCollision bodies of meshes, invisible blockers, the terrain cells with holes left open)
and every Character body (PLAYERCLIP); it never contains a Raycast body (railings, glass and foliage shells, see-through
detail), a part only a later health state shows (the soup drops those), or an object the level starts hidden (listed,
not built). Foliage and scatter enter only when their Havok body is Collision, and then the motor collides with them
too.

`sim.nav` proves the equality on Metro CQL and Caspian CQL, three ways. Revision 1's proof 1 compared two digests
computed from the same soup through the same `static_layer_of`, which is close to a tautology (review); proof 1 now
starts from the cooked Jolt shapes, the thing the motor actually queries, and proof 3 stays the geometric check.

1. **Per nav tile, against the cooked world.** `nav_check_input_against_world` reads the triangles back out of the
   static world's cooked MeshShapes (`Shape::GetTrianglesStart/Next` over the tile's input box, on the Collision and
   Character shapes of every static tile whose `StaticTileInfo` bounds overlap the box, each triangle kept when its
   own XZ bounds overlap) and matches them one to one with the tile's nav input within 1 mm per corner (Jolt keeps
   vertices quantised to 21 bits over a shape's bounds, ≈ 0.1 mm on a 200 m tile, so the match is by tolerance).
   Gate: `world_only` = 0 on every tile of both levels, and `nav_only` = 0 except triangles Jolt's cook drops as
   degenerate (`MeshShapeSettings` sanitises them, static_world.cpp; they have no area to collide with or rasterise),
   which the check recomputes with Jolt's own rule and counts apart. It catches a nav that misses long triangles
   (§5.3) and one that takes a triangle the motor does not collide with; it would have failed revision 1's 3 × 3
   gather on Metro's 112 long triangles. As a cross-check, each static tile's per-layer counts equal
   `StaticTileInfo::triangles`, and its `content` digest equals the digest recomputed from the soup tile.
2. **No foreign layer.** The input holds 0 Raycast triangles; the report lists the movement-set triangles by surface
   class (foliage and glass counts included), so what the motor collides with is visible.
3. **By geometry (the substantive check).** 10,000 seeded rays (vertical drops and horizontal eye-height rays inside
   the region), plus one vertical ray onto each of the long triangles of §4 at a point at least 64 m from its own
   tile: the first hit of `CollisionWorld::ray(…, kMovement)` equals a brute-force double-precision ray test against
   the nav input triangles of the tile the ray lies in, within 1 mm (ambiguous grazing hits counted, not judged, as in
   `sim.world`).

### 5.2 Terrain cover: the motor needs the ground the nav walks on

The default soup keeps terrain cells only in tiles holding Havok triangles and their 8 neighbours (collision_soup.h).
That leaves combat-area ground without collision: 35 of Caspian CQL's 273 combat-area tiles and 1,119 of Kharg's
1,494 hold no soup triangle at all (§4). A walker sent there would fall through the world, and a nav baked "over the
combat areas" from that soup would have holes the bots route around. So S1 adds `SoupOptions::terrain_cover`: extra
tiles whose terrain cells are kept as well. S1 passes the level's combat-area tiles (§5.3) to both the static world
(`StaticWorldLoadOptions::terrain_cover`, `SharedLoadOptions::terrain_cover`) and the nav, so the motor and the nav see
the same ground.

* An empty cover gives the M1 soup bit for bit. The level-oracle parity (230 runs) and M1's tests and cache entries are
  untouched, and `kCollisionSoupVersion` stays 1.
* A non-empty cover adds `cover=<SHA-256 of the sorted tile list>` to the "jolt-world" key. Tiles are content keyed
  anyway; most tiles hash identically with and without the cover, so they are shared.
* **Deep water [inferred, to measure].** Kharg's cover reaches the sea bottom. S1 has no swimming (the motor's
  Swimming is a stub, m1.md §6.9), so walkers on the sea bed would be wrong but harmless. The cover takes a combat-area
  tile only when at least one of its terrain samples lies above the water plane − 1.8 m (one soldier height). The plane
  is the WaterEntityData's transform y (0 on Kharg). If that height proves wrong, the cover keeps every tile and the
  sea bed stays walkable for S1 (G4 timing is then measured on the larger set; reported).

### 5.3 Bake

* **Region.** `level_combat_tiles` traverses every MP mode of the level, reads its combat areas (`read_mode_areas`) and
  takes the union of the 64 m tiles their polygons, grown by `region_margin` 32 m (gap load §2.7), touch (plan §3.6:
  "bake per level over the union of its modes' combat areas"). Every mode of the level then bakes the same tiles, and a
  tile whose input is the same in two modes is one cache entry.
* **Point tiles** (review of revision 2). The region also takes the tiles within the margin of every objective (flags,
  HQs, MCOMs) and every AlternateSpawn point of every mode (`point_tiles`; `read_level_spawns` in the same traversal),
  so a point outside every combat polygon is still baked and G2 judges it for a nav reason. The reviewer checked
  Caspian CQL/RL/TDM: the AlternateSpawn and CapturePoint bounds (x −394..385, z −371..809) lie inside the CQL combat
  extents. `LevelCombatTiles` prints `points_outside_areas` and `point_only_tiles` (0 expected). These tiles are part
  of the terrain cover too (§5.2), so the motor has ground there.
* **Tiles.** The soup's 64 m grid; tile (x, z) covers [64x, 64x + 64) × [64z, 64z + 64).
  * **Input rule.** A tile's input is every movement-layer triangle of the soup, filed under any soup tile, whose XZ
    bounding box overlaps the tile's input box: the square grown by `nav_input_border` (nav.h) on each side. The soup
    files a triangle under its centroid's tile and never clips it, and the static world's tile MeshShape keeps the
    whole triangle, so the motor collides with a long triangle everywhere it lies. Revision 1 took only the 3 × 3
    soup tiles around a nav tile, which misses 112 Collision triangles on Metro and 154 on Kharg, up to 205 m long (§4,
    measured): holes in the nav that the motor does not have, which would have been blamed on objects in §5.7.
  * **Gather.** One pass over the soup: each movement triangle's box gives a tile range, clamped to the requested
    tiles' range, and the triangle is filed under every requested tile in it; each (triangle, tile) pair is charged
    against `kMaxNavInputRefs` (§9). Work is linear in the soup plus the pairs (a normal triangle has 1-4). Order is
    soup order, so the digest is deterministic.
  * **Border.** `nav_input_border` = max(Recast's border, walkable radius + 3 cells = 1.25 m; the drop-link sweep's
    reach, `drop_reach` + radius + 1 cell = 1.6 m) = **1.6 m** at the defaults. The Recast heightfield border stays
    walkable radius + 3 cells (Sample_TileMesh), so neighbouring tiles' borders match at the seams. The wider input box
    only makes the digest cover every triangle the link sweeps can touch (review: a 1.0 m reach plus the 0.35 m
    capsule went 1.35 m past a 1.25 m border, so two modes could share a tile entry whose link checks differ).
  * **Raster charge.** Recast clips each triangle to the heightfield, so the cell charge of §9 is the triangle's box
    clipped to the tile's heightfield box, not its whole box (a 200 m floor slab costs one tile's cells per tile).
  * The heightfield's y range is the input's, capped at `kMaxNavTileHeight` (§9). Tiles bake independently and in
    parallel.
* **Parameters** [plan §3.6; soldier verified §4]: cs 0.25, ch 0.1; walkable climb 0.3 m = 3 cells, so climb / ch is
  whole (topics/06 §2.4: ch 0.3 broke Metro's stairs); slope 50°; radius 0.35 m. The climb's cell count is
  `lround(climb / ch)`, and `check_nav_params` refuses a climb that is not whole within 10⁻³ cells (Recast's sample
  floors, and 0.3f / 0.1f only happens to land just above 3: a tuning value of 0.29999 would floor to 2 cells).
  * **Radius rounding.** At cs 0.25 the radius is 2 cells = 0.5 m with Recast's `ceil` (`radius_round_up`, the default
    and what gap load §2.7 measured), or 1 cell with round-to-nearest. 0.5 m erodes 0.15 m more than the capsule on each
    side, so a 0.9 m doorway that the capsule fits (0.7 m) closes in the nav. The gate runs with `ceil`. When §5.7
    finds a split at a passage between 0.7 and 1.0 m wide, the spike re-runs G2 with 1 cell and reports both. 1 cell
    is acceptable because the corridor's corners stay at least 0.25 m off walls and the motor slides along the rest
    (m1.md §6.1).
  * **Height.** The walkable height is the crouch height, 1.15 m = 12 cells. After `rcBuildCompactHeightfield`, every
    span with less than the stand height of room (1.8 m = 18 cells) gets area `kNavAreaCrouch`, so crouch-only
    passages are in the mesh and the brain crouches through them. Prone-only space (< 1.15 m) is not walkable.
  * **Recast settings.** Watershed partitioning (`NavPartition::Watershed`, the Recast sample's default and gap load
    §2.7's); max edge 12 m; simplification error 1.3; min / merge region 8² / 20² cells; detail sample distance 6
    cells, error 1 ch; 6 verts per poly (Detour's maximum).
* **Drop links** (topics/06 §2.6 item 1). Each border edge of a tile's poly mesh (an edge without a neighbour) is
  sampled every 1 m. A landing within `drop_reach` 1.0 m outward and at most `drop_max` 4.9 m lower (IW4L's 192 in; a
  4.9 m fall lands at 9.8 m/s, below the 13.9 m/s damage knee) becomes a one-way off-mesh link (`kNavAreaDrop`,
  `nav_flags::kDrop`). The landing is found against the tile's own input, so links are tile-local and content-keyed.
  A stand-capsule sweep (kMovement) on the lane's checked-out CollisionWorld over `link_shapes` (§3) must find the drop
  path clear: out over the edge by `drop_reach`, then down to the landing. Everything the sweep can touch lies inside
  the input box (border rule above), so the tile's input digest plus "links=jolt" and the Jolt build id in the key
  (§5.4) determine the result. The gates and the game always check (link_shapes = the S1 static world); only synthetic
  unit tests bake without (`links=none`, a different key). Jump links
  (gaps, low walls up to the 0.6 m jump) and ladders are not built in S1 (§13).
* **64-bit poly refs** (`RECASTNAVIGATION_DT_POLYREF64 ON`, top-level CMakeLists S1 line). With 32-bit refs a
  4,096-tile mesh leaves 10 bits for polys per tile (1,024), and Kharg's CQL alone touches 1,494 combat-area tiles.
  Dense 64 m tiles of Metro's station can exceed 1,024 polys [inferred].
* **Assembly.** `dtNavMesh::init` (tile size 64 m, `maxTiles` = next power of two of the baked tiles, origin (0, 0, 0)
  so Detour's tile coordinates are the soup's), then `addTile` in (x, z) order.
* **Cost** [plan §3.6, gap load §2.7: BF3 capsule on the level_bf3 soup, 16 threads]: Caspian 5.18 s, Kharg 11.84 s,
  Metro TDM 0.17 s; tiles of terrain only are cheap. The gates (G4) are those numbers plus margin. Our regions differ
  from the research's (combat union over all modes, the terrain cover, water rule), so G4 prints the tile counts
  (terrain-only and object tiles) next to the times.

### 5.4 Cache

Through the existing `Cache` and `cached_convert` (m1.md §5.5 conventions; engine = `cache_engine_id()`, converter
`kNavConverterVersion`, format `kNavFormatVersion`, sources empty: the digests stand for them).

| kind | key params | payload |
|---|---|---|
| `nav-tile` | `input=<NavTileInput::digest hex>;` (SHA-256 over the tile key first, then every input triangle's float bits and layer; the tile key must stay, since one slab can be the whole input of two tiles and the Detour data carries the tile's coordinates) + `nav_params_text` + `;` + `recast_build_id()` (`recast=1.6.0;polyref64=1;verts_per_poly=6`) + `;links=jolt` with `jolt_version = jolt_build_id()` when the drop links were sweep-checked, else `;links=none` | "OBF3NTIL" v1: NavTileInfo, then the Detour tile data (`dtCreateNavMeshData` output) |
| `nav-world` | `source=<static world source digest>;level=<lower>;mode=<lower>;soup=<kCollisionSoupVersion>;terrain_spacing=<f32 bits>;cover=<SHA-256 of the cover tiles>;region=<SHA-256 of the region tiles>;` + `nav_params_text` + recast id; same versions | "OBF3NWLD" v1: per tile x, z, input digest, NavTileInfo |
| `nav-area` | `level=<lower>;margin=<f32 bits>;modes=<mode list>;sources=<SHA-256 over every mode's source digest>` | "OBF3NARA" v1: the region tiles and, per mode, its objectives and combat polygons |

* **Plan deviation (recorded).** Plan §6 S1 / §3.6 name one cache "kind nav; key = collision digest + params + recast
  version + converter version". S1 refines it into three kinds: per-tile entries keyed by the tile's input digest
  (the "collision digest" of exactly what the tile rasterises and sweeps), the params, the Recast build id and the
  converter version (all four of the plan's parts); a per level × mode index; and the combat-area region. Reason:
  the 7 modes of a level share most tiles, and a per-tile key is what M6's dirty-tile rebake needs (§13).
* **Warm path.** The static world's source digest → `nav-world` → every `nav-tile` → `validate_detour_tile` →
  `addTile`. No soup, no Recast.
* **Cold path.** The soup (the static world's cold path already built it; else `load_nav_world_cached` builds it) →
  `nav_tile_inputs` → per tile a hit by input digest or a bake and put → the world entry last, only when every tile was
  stored (as `jolt-world`).
* `nav-area` needs every mode's source digest, so a warm read still mounts each mode (≈ 0.2 s each, reported). An
  unavailable digest only costs the shortcut.

### 5.5 Connectivity (G2)

* **Graph.** The polys of every tile, linked by Detour's links: internal neighbours and tile portals, which are both
  ways, and off-mesh drop links, which are one way (start → link → landing). `nav_components` computes the **strongly
  connected components** (iterative Tarjan, O(polys + links)). A bot must be able to reach every objective from every
  spawn and come back, which one weakly connected piece with one-way drops does not guarantee. The weak count is
  printed as well. The main component is the largest by walkable area.
* **Rows.** One per flag, HQ, MCOM (mode_areas.h) and AlternateSpawn point (spawn_points.h) of the mode, enabled or
  not (the mode's logic enables them during a round).
  * A spawn is checked at its **settled feet**: `settle_spawn` on the S1 static world, the position the server
    spawns at. If the settle fails, the raw point is used and the row says so.
  * `nav_check_point` takes the nearest poly in a box around the point.
  * A row is **on the mesh** when the horizontal offset is at most the kind's radius:
    * spawn: 1.0 m, |vertical| ≤ 1.0 m. The settle shifts up to 0.5 m, and the 0.5 m erosion of §5.3 can put a
      spawn 0.35 m from a wall just off the mesh;
    * MCOM: 2.0 m. The arm radius is 1.5 m [verified], plus the erosion;
    * flag and HQ: 5.0 m [inferred]. A capture point's transform is the flag pole, which has collision of its own;
      a soldier only has to stand in the zone.
* **Gate.** Every row of every mode on the mesh and in the main component. The row prints kind, index, position,
  horizontal and vertical offset, its component's id, poly count and area, and its **weak component**
  (`NavComponents::weak_component`, `NavPointCheck::weak_component`, `BotPointRow::in_main_weak`).
* **Failure classes.** The plan says "one connected nav component"; strong connectivity is stricter, since a spawn
  whose only way out is a one-way drop fails it although bots can leave it. A failing row is classed *one-way only*
  (in the main SCC's weak component but not in the SCC: it needs a link back, or the drop is the only way in) or
  *disconnected* (another weak component: a split, §5.7). The report counts both classes per mode; R6's response
  (§14) is chosen from them.

### 5.6 Queries

`NavQuery` (nav_query.h): one dtNavMeshQuery with a 4,096-node pool per instance (topics/06 §2.4's harness used a
65,535-node pool for full-map queries; the bots' sliced searches need far fewer per step, and a search that runs out
returns a partial path that the brain repaths from). It offers nearest poly, random point inside a component, and
sliced A* (begin / continue with an iteration budget / finish, at most 512 polys). `NavCorridor` wraps dtPathCorridor:
`move_position` follows the agent along the surface and reports when it left the corridor, and `corners` gives up to
8 straight-path corners with the area and flags of the poly each corner leaves into.

### 5.7 The Metro split: how S1 finds and fixes it

Research: with the BF3 capsule the largest component holds 43.4 % (IW4L soup, topics/06 C11) and 23 % (level_bf3
soup, gap load §2.7). The objects at the split points are SOLID 2–4.6 m tall shapes standing on the floor, at (−171.5,
−258.9), (−131.7, −235.3) and (−150.2, −273.2), and a 2.4 m SOLID + GLASS object at (−172, 59.3, −259) that the human
trace walks through. The cause is open. The prime suspect is the research soups treating Raycast bodies as SOLID
(handoff 04 §3.2). The gap load report calls the split "not an IW4L-soup artefact" because it reproduces on the
level_bf3 soup, but that soup has the same rule [verified: IW4L `crates/level_bf3/src/collision/mod.rs`
`build_with`: "a raycast body ... is `SOLID`; a collision body is `SOLID` only when its object has no raycast body"],
so the reproduction does not clear the suspect. Our soup keeps every body with its kind and the motor ignores
Raycast. The procedure:

1. **Bake twice:** with `layer_mask = kMovement` (the gate's input) and with `kAll` (Raycast included, the research's
   situation). `bf3dump nav components MP_Subway ConquestLarge0 --layers=movement|all` prints the components (polys,
   area, bounds) and, for the two largest, their **seams**: pairs of polys of different components within 1.0 m
   horizontally and 0.5 m vertically. Those are the split points.
2. **Name the objects at each seam:** `bf3dump nav probe <x> <y> <z> <radius>` lists the soup placements with
   triangles within the radius of the point: placement kind, entity, res, body kind, surface, flags, entity type and
   triangle count.
3. **Decide by the data, one rule per object class, the same for the motor and the nav:**
   * a Raycast body: already out of both sets (§5.1);
   * an object BF3 soldiers pass but our static world builds solid (a gate or door that starts open, a prop that is a
     dynamic body in BF3, a part a later state shows): it leaves **both** sets through a soup classification, a
     tag flag plus `static_layer_of`, never through the nav alone, so §5.1's equality holds. The evidence (entity
     type, Physics data, name) is written in §15;
   * a real obstacle our soldier also cannot pass (a kiosk, a counter): the split is real for our sim, and the gate
     needs a link (a jump link over a ≤ 0.6 m obstacle) or the point is reported as unreachable with its object.
4. The result table (split points, objects, decision, component share before and after) goes into this section's
   "As built".

**As built:** see §19.4 for the variants table and §19.3 for the named objects. The radius rounding (cs 0.25 with ceil
gives 0.5 m) was the main cause: Metro CQL G2 rows in main went from 40/78 to 69/78 at cs 0.2. Raycast bodies are not
a cause.

## 6. Server scale

### 6.1 What one tick contains, and how it is measured

A GameServer tick (game_server.h order, M1 §8.1), as `run_scale_bench` times it with `steady_clock` around each part
(`GameServerTickTimes`, game_server.h). The bench reads it after every tick through `GameServer::last_tick_times()`,
a reference: `stats()` would copy the 128-entry slot list every tick and pollute the caches between ticks.
`GameServerStats` copies the same fields for ServerHost.

**Every part is assigned on every tick and is 0 when its step did not run.** M1 assigns `last_encode_ms` only inside
`send_snapshots`, so with `snapshot_divisor` 2 every odd tick reports the previous tick's encode. M1's field keeps that
meaning (no consumer reads it); `GameServerTickTimes::encode_ms` is the per-tick value, so window means, shares and the
"parts sum to the whole" unit check are exact (review of revision 2).

| part | content | field |
|---|---|---|
| commands | per slot, the command for this tick from the jitter buffer, else the repeat (the observer's hold command) | `commands_ms` |
| command sources | `PathService::run` (sliced A* steps, ≤ `iterations_per_tick` across all bots; its iterations, ticks at the budget and longest run are counted, `PathServiceStats`), then every bot's `WalkerBrain::command` in slot order: per tick a cheap steering step (no nav query); on the bot's staggered think tick one `NavCorridor::move_position`, the corners, arrival / stuck checks, and on a new goal a `random_point` or nearest-poly query and a path request | `sources_ms` (of which `paths_ms`) |
| motor | one `SoldierMotor::step` per live soldier (65): CharacterVirtual reset + `RefreshContacts` + `ExtendedUpdate` (sweeps, stair walk) + the motor's ground hug and pose-fit queries (m1.md §6.1) | `step_ms`, `motor_steps`, `collision_queries` |
| rules | respawns (each a `settle_spawn`: 60 motor steps), dev requests (none in the bench) | `rules_ms`, `spawns` |
| snapshots | every second tick, one snapshot per observer: own state + up to ≈ 55 others nearest first (the 1,200 B cap, m1.md §7.3) | `encode_ms` (0 between snapshots), `snapshots` |
| whole | `GameServer::tick` | `tick_ms` |

* **Back to back.** The bench calls `GameServer::tick` back to back on one thread: no sleeps, no ENet service (the
  datagram path's cost is ENet's, outside GameServer). So a tick's wall time is its single-thread CPU time, the
  quantity a 60 Hz ServerHost thread must fit into 16.7 ms and the gate into 8 ms.
* **Known optimistic bias.** Back to back, the caches stay warm and the core stays boosted; a real ServerHost sleeps
  between ticks, so its p99 can be higher than the bench's. After the gate window the bench runs a **paced row**
  (`paced_seconds` = 30 s of sim time, each tick started on a 16.667 ms boundary with a sleep in between, as
  ServerHost does) and prints its percentiles beside the gate. It is not gated, but a paced p99 well above the gate's
  says the gate is optimistic, and the acceptance rerun should look at both.
* **Noise evidence.** The bench thread runs at `THREAD_PRIORITY_ABOVE_NORMAL` during the windows (its own thread only)
  and records its CPU time (`GetThreadTimes`) beside the window's wall time: `cpu_share` < 0.95 flags the run as
  preempted ("noisy") in the report. RUN_SERIAL keeps out the other ctests, not the other worktrees' builds.
* **Spawns.** `add_local_soldier` spawns at once (game_server.cpp: it calls `spawn()`, which settles), and
  `GameServer::init` adds the bots, so the 64 initial settles happen inside `init`, not in any tick [verified]. The
  bench times `init` as `seconds_init`; `spawn_tick_ms` covers respawns in the warm-up only (normally empty). A
  respawn inside the window (a fall death; drops are ≤ 4.9 m, so none is expected) stays in the percentiles and is
  counted.
* **Timer edits.** The tick-part timers in `GameServer::run_tick` are single added lines around the existing steps
  (one `steady_clock` read per boundary), no restructuring: s4 (level logic) will likely add a step to the same
  function, and small separate lines keep that merge trivial.
* **Mode.** ConquestLarge0 on both levels: the 64-player mode, with 73 (Metro) and 155 (Caspian) spawn points, so 64
  spawns find points (TDM Caspian has 65). Bots alternate teams 1 and 2.
* **Encode scaling.** The bench also runs (printed, not gated) with `observers = 16`, to show what encoding for more
  peers costs. M3's gate (64 connections, tick < 8 ms) is M3's.

### 6.2 Budget expectations [plan, inferred]

Plan §3.5: server tick 4–6 ms of 16.7 [inferred]; topics/06 §3.9 / C17: bots ≈ 0.3–0.9 ms per tick single-threaded,
dominated by perception rays, which walkers do not cast; motor ≈ 64 × 1–2 µs per tick [inferred] against M1's
measured ≈ 2 µs per step on a busy synthetic course and real Metro geometry unmeasured. So G1 is expected to pass
with margin; the spike's value is the measured split.

### 6.3 Sweep cost (G3)

* `CollisionWorld::query_count()` counts the world's narrow-phase queries. There is one broadphase layer, and Jolt's
  BroadPhaseQuadTree calls `ShouldCollide(BroadPhaseLayer)` exactly once per ray, cast or collide query [verified:
  BroadPhaseQuadTree.cpp, every query loops the layers once]. So a counting filter in `CollisionWorld::Impl`'s per-mask
  broadphase filters counts every CharacterVirtual sweep, contact collect, stair probe and the motor's own queries.
  The motor passes `w.broadphase_filter(mask)` to `SetShape`, `RefreshContacts`, `ExtendedUpdate` and `StickToFloor`
  (soldier_motor.cpp), so no CharacterVirtual query bypasses it [verified, review]. The quadtree evaluates
  `tree.HasBodies() && ShouldCollide(...)`, so a query against an empty layer is not counted: exact on real levels,
  and `host.scale.unit` never asserts `query_count > 0` on a world without static bodies.
* The report: motor steps, queries per step, queries per second of sim time at 64 walkers (plan R1's estimate:
  64 × 5 × 60 ≈ 19 k/s), µs per step (`step_ms` / soldiers), and `us_per_query_upper` = µs per step / queries per
  step. That last one is an **upper bound**: the CharacterVirtual solver's own math is inside the step time, and
  Jolt's internal casts cannot be timed one by one from outside.
* **Direct query cost.** So the fallback rule does not decide on an upper bound alone, the bench records up to
  `sweep_samples` = 4,096 bot feet positions and velocities during the window and, after it, replays each on the
  bench's own `CollisionWorld`, created over `SharedLevel::static_world` (the same shapes the server's world holds;
  GameServer's world is private and gets no accessor), as one `sweep_capsule` (stand capsule, 1/60 s of sprint along the velocity) and one
  `overlap_capsule`, timing each call alone (the timer's own cost, measured on empty calls, subtracted). Mean and p99
  µs (`us_sweep`, `us_overlap`). These are our own queries on our MeshShapes, the operations an own BVH would
  replace, so they are what §14's BVH branch reads.

### 6.4 Detour costs and nav memory

* From the server NavQuery's counters (`NavQueryStats`): calls and mean µs of sliced paths (init + updates +
  finalize), corridor moves and nearest-poly queries, per second of sim time.
* `bf3dump nav path` runs 1,000 seeded full A* queries between random points of the main component and prints mean /
  p95 / max ms and path lengths (research: Metro 0.10–0.11 ms mean, Caspian 0.28 / 1.69 ms, Kharg 0.92 / 4.48 ms).
* Path service: A* iterations per second, `us_per_iteration` (`ms_run` / iterations, an upper bound: init and
  finalize are inside), the share of ticks whose `run()` spent the whole budget with requests left
  (`ticks_at_budget_share`), and the longest `run()`. At 0.5-1 µs per iteration a saturated 4,096 budget would cost
  2-4 ms per tick [inferred, review], half the gate, so these numbers decide §14's budget step.
* Memory: `NavTileInfo::data_bytes` per tile (mean, max) and in total, plus the peak working set of the bake.

## 7. Walker brain (bots/walker_brain.h)

* **Contract.** A brain reads a `WalkerView`: its own authoritative state after tick − 1, the tick, the goal set
  and the components, all immutable, plus the NavQuery and PathService of its thread, which are mutable query
  services (Detour's node pool, the request queue), not sim state. It returns the `Command` for the tick, the same
  struct a human's client sends. It never writes sim state, never teleports, never reads another soldier. That is
  plan §3.6 "reads an immutable view, writes Commands only" and D11.
* **Moving to a pool** (deferred, §13) needs one NavQuery and one PathService per worker; the single
  first-come-first-served queue becomes one per worker, so request order and the command hash change. Determinism
  then holds for a fixed bot-to-worker assignment (by slot), not against the serial run.
* **Goals.** Every goal is in the main strongly connected component, so every goal is reachable from every bot on it.
  Bots spawn on it (§8 "Bot spawns"). A bot whose path request finds its start poly in another component
  (`nav_poly_component`: a fall, or a spawn the filter could not avoid) counts `starts_outside_main`, drops its goal
  and draws its goals from its own component from then on (random points only: objectives are main-component rows);
  a new life resets it to main. A shortest path between two main-component polys never leaves the component (a poly
  on it is reachable from main and reaches main), so walking bots stay in it.
  * `objective_share` of the goals are mode objectives: flags, HQs and MCOMs, plus spawn points so TDM / SQDM have
    objectives.
  * The rest are `random_point`s at least 20 m away.
  * Arrival: within 1.5 m horizontally and 1.5 m vertically.
* **Path.** A `PathService` request; the answer, Done or Partial, becomes a `NavCorridor`. A Partial or Failed answer
  counts and is repathed from the corridor's end, or the goal is dropped after `max_repaths`.
* **Steering, every tick** (no nav query):
  * the target is the next corner, or the one after it once within `corner_lookahead`;
  * yaw: turned towards the target at most 540°/s, i.e. 9° per tick (research topics/06 §1: ≈ 550°/s);
  * move axes: the remaining direction in the yaw frame, `quantize_axis(cos)` forward and `quantize_axis(sin)` right,
    so a bot walks while it turns;
  * pose: `Crouch` when the current or next poly is `kNavAreaCrouch`, or during a crouch leg (`crouch_share` of the
    legs), else `Stand`;
  * sprint: on a sprint leg (`sprint_share`), when standing, on walk area, and the next corner is ≥ 6 m straight ahead
    within 20°. Never sprint while crouched, which would stand the soldier up (`sprint_stands_up`).
* **Think**, staggered: period = round(60 / think_hz) = 6 ticks at 10 Hz, phase = slot % period, so about 11 of the 64
  bots think per tick. A think runs `move_position` to the bot's position (repath when it left the corridor), refreshes
  the corners, and checks arrival, progress and stuck.
* **Stuck.** Movement commanded but less than 0.5 m of progress over 2 s (IW4L `STUCK_TICKS` 40 at 20 Hz) → repath
  from the current position; after 3 in a row, a new goal (counted as abandoned). A bot with no poly within 2 m walks
  back to the nearest poly (`off_mesh_ticks`). All counted in `WalkerStats`; G1's validity condition reads them.
* **Determinism.** Per-bot splitmix64 seeded from the match seed and the slot, brains in slot order, PathService first
  come first served: a bench run reproduces (host.scale.unit checks a hash over every command).

## 8. GameServer integration (bot_fill.h)

* **Config.** `GameServerConfig::bots` (additive). `GameServer::init` adds `count` server-side soldiers with
  `add_local_soldier`, teams 1 and 2 alternating, each a `CommandSource` forwarding to its WalkerBrain. That is the
  path the M1 scripted walkers take, so the motor, the settle, snapshots and stats are unchanged; only the list a bot
  spawns from differs (next item).
* **Bot spawns** (review of revision 2: G1 must not depend on G2). `load_bot_level` builds `BotLevel::spawns`
  (`BotSpawns`, bot_fill.h) from the Spawn rows:
  1. every spawn point whose settled feet are in the main component, with its team;
  2. if team 1 or 2 then has no point of its own team or neutral, every point of step 1 becomes neutral
     (`teams_merged`; S1 has no combat, so sides do not matter);
  3. if step 1 found nothing, `count` seeded random points of the main component, settled, as neutral points
     (`synthetic`).

  With `BotFillConfig::spawn_in_main` (the default; the bench and host.scale) `GameServer::spawn()` runs
  `choose_spawn` over that list for bot slots, initial spawns and respawns alike, and the slot's spawn index refers to
  it; M1's fallback when every point is near a live soldier (a seeded point anyway) draws from the same list, so
  a short list (Metro's main component may hold only a few points) never sends a bot elsewhere. Humans and M1's
  scripted walkers keep `SharedLevel::spawns`. The report prints the level's points, those in main,
  the excluded ones, `teams_merged` and `synthetic`, so the G1 run says how far the Metro split constrained it. The
  Godot run may pass `spawn_in_main = false` to show bots where humans spawn.
* **Director.** One per GameServer (bot_fill.cpp) owns the server thread's NavQuery and PathService. The first
  `CommandSource::command` call of a tick runs `PathService::run`, so GameServer's tick needs no new step.
* **Stats.** GameServer copies the director's `BotFillStats` and `last_tick_times()` into `GameServerStats`
  (additive fields; ServerHost publishes them unchanged).
* **Loading.** `load_bot_level` runs on the loader after `load_shared_level`, which must have used the same terrain
  cover. `SharedLevel::terrain_cover` (additive, level_load.h) records the cover the static world was built with;
  `load_bot_level` compares it with the level's combat-area tiles and fails on a mismatch. The bake's link checks use
  `SharedLevel::static_world` as `link_shapes`.
* **Compile surface.** `game_server.h` includes only `bot_fill_stats.h` (POD counters over `bots/bot_stats.h`) and
  forward-declares `BotFillConfig`; `nav.h`, `nav_query.h` and `walker_brain.h` stay out of M1's includers
  (bf3_godot, the tests). `game_server.cpp` includes `bot_fill.h`.
* **Slots.** `max_slots` must hold the bots, the walkers and the peers: the bench uses 128, the game with `--bots`
  128.

## 9. Hostile-input bounds

Install data and cache blobs are hostile. Every rule fails with an error, never a crash, hang or unbounded allocation.

* **mode_areas** (bf3_core): every array, connection and name it scans or compares charges the World's `WorkBudget`
  before it is used (the M0 rule). At most 256 combat areas of 4,096 points each. `combat_area_tiles` tests each
  candidate tile against every edge (tiles × edge samples): it sums that product over the areas before the loop and
  fails past `kMaxCombatAreaTileWork` = 2^26 (a retail area is about 10^5), and it refuses non-finite points and points
  beyond 10^6 m. `point_tiles` gives at most 4 tiles per point (margin clamped to 64 m).
* **nav_tile_inputs**: at most 2^21 input triangles per tile; at most 2^26 (triangle, tile) pairs in the gather, each
  charged, a triangle's tile range clamped to the requested range before it is walked (a hostile 10 km triangle
  costs the requested tiles it overlaps, never its own extent); non-finite corners are already dropped by the soup.
* **Recast, per tile:**
  * before rasterising, the sum over its triangles of the cells their XZ bounding boxes cover, clipped to the
    tile's heightfield box (what Recast rasterises), is charged against 2^28. A soup of huge overlapping triangles fails the tile instead of allocating spans without end. The rest of
    Recast's pipeline (compact heightfield, distance field, regions, contours, poly mesh, detail) is linear in the
    spans or bounded by the region count;
  * the y range is capped at 800 m (13-bit span heights at ch 0.1);
  * a Recast step that fails names the tile;
  * at most 2^16 tiles per world.
* **Detour tile data from the cache.** `dtNavMesh::addTile` checks only the magic and the version. So
  `validate_detour_tile` recomputes Detour 1.6.0's layout from the header counts (the aligned sections of
  `dtCreateNavMeshData`) against the data size, then checks every index before Detour sees it:
  * poly vertex indices < vertCount; neis 0, an external-link flag, or ≤ polyCount; vertCount per poly ≤ 6;
  * detail meshes inside the detail arrays; BV nodes inside the tree;
  * off-mesh connection polys inside the tile; the tile coordinates equal the key's tile;
  * finite coordinates inside the header bounds.

  It mirrors Detour's private constants; a Recast bump changes `recast_build_id()` and with it every key. The
  `sim.nav.unit` fuzz mutates 2,000 real tile blobs; each is refused, or added and queried without fault (also under
  asan).
* **Blobs.** Every count is checked against the bytes left before anything is allocated; trailing bytes are refused.
* **Link checks.** One sweep per drop candidate, candidates ≤ one per 1 m of border edge, the poly mesh's edges
  bounded by its own limits; the per-lane world pool holds at most `threads` worlds.
* **Queries.** Node pools are fixed per NavQuery, paths ≤ 512 polys, corners ≤ 8; sliced searches run within their
  iteration budget; the brain clamps its axes and yaw to the Command's ranges.

**Fallback rule (plan: 30 Hz sim, fewer sweeps, own BVH behind ICollisionWorld).** If G1 fails on a level, the
bench's split decides.

* **Share, defined.** Over the measured ticks whose `last_tick_ms` ≥ the p99 (the slowest 1 %, 72 of 7,200), each
  part's mean divided by those ticks' mean `last_tick_ms` (`ScaleReport::tail`, `TailShares`). Not the ratio of each
  part's own p99 to the tick's p99: a part's p99 may fall on other ticks than the slow ones. The whole-window mean
  shares are printed beside it.
* **Branches, in order; the first that applies is tried, then `host.scale` is rerun:**
  1. **Motor share ≥ 50 %** (`step`):
     * **fewer sweeps** first, always: `max_collision_iterations` 5 → 3 (the only sweep-count key the motor has:
       motor_tuning.cpp; soldier_motor.cpp:423 sets `mWalkStairsStepUp` every grounded step, and Jolt's
       `ExtendedUpdate` already runs the stair walk only when the horizontal move was blocked, so there is no stair
       key to turn; a rule that skips stair walks would be a motor change, not tuning, and needs sim.motor E1 again).
       Accepted when p99 ≤ 8 ms and `sim.motor` still passes (E1 unchanged);
     * else, **own BVH** when the *direct* query cost (`us_sweep` mean, §6.3) × queries per step is ≥ 50 % of
       `us_per_step`: the queries themselves dominate the step on our MeshShapes. Not built in S1; the doc records
       the direct µs, the queries per step and the share of the tick an X-times-faster query would save. The upper
       bound `us_per_query_upper` is printed beside it, never used alone for this decision;
     * else **30 Hz** (below).
  2. **Sources share ≥ 30 %** (brains + nav): if `ticks_at_budget_share` > 5 % or `PathServiceStats::ms_run` is most
     of the sources time, lower `iterations_per_tick` to 2,048 (from the measured µs per iteration: the budget that
     costs ≤ 1 ms); then `think_hz` 10 → 5; then brains on a job pool (one NavQuery and PathService per worker, §7).
  3. **Encode share ≥ 30 %**: priority-limited encoding (M3's ghost priority); the `observers = 16` row says how it
     scales.
  4. **Default, when no part reaches its threshold** (e.g. motor 45 %, sources 25 %, encode 20 %): **30 Hz**, the
     plan's first fallback, because it halves every per-second cost at once. 30 Hz means two constants, which must
     stay equal (server_host.h requires `tick_hz == kSimHz`): `sim::kSimHz` (sim_types.h) and
     `PhysicsWorld::kTickHz` (physics_world.h). Rebuild with both at 30 and rerun the same bench; the reading is
     then "p99 ≤ 8 ms per 30 Hz tick". The motor's 30 Hz E1 behaviour is S5 harness H:T1's question, not S1's.
* The doc's §6 "As built" gets the table of measured shares (tail and mean), the variant tried and its p99, and the
  recommendation.

### 19.2 What changed from revision 3

* **Cell size 0.2 m, radius rounded up (2 cells = 0.4 m)**, not the plan's 0.25 (nav.h). Recast erodes in whole cells
  and the conservative raster grows each wall by up to one more cell. At 0.25 with ceil the radius is 0.5 m and closes
  the 0.7–1.0 m passages the motor walks; with round-to-nearest it is 0.25 m and the corridors pass gaps the 0.35 m
  capsule does not, so in G1 half of the goals were abandoned (an invalid run). 0.2 with ceil keeps both. The §5.7
  table shows the effect.
* **Detail sample error 2 ch (0.2 m)**, not 1 ch. `rcBuildPolyMeshDetail` was 76 % of Caspian's bake CPU at 1 ch
  (G4 6.2 s at cs 0.2) and G4 is 2.8–3.9 s at 2 ch. Monotone and layer partitions were tried (`--partition`) and did
  not help. The detail mesh only sets the heights queries return; the motor stands on the physics world.
* **The water rule is built** (§5.2): `read_water_planes` (mode_areas.h) reads the LakeData shapes under each
  WaterEntityData's Asset. Kharg has one 8.8 km plane at y 88.47; Metro's pond is at 62.56; Caspian's lake is at
  67.26, under the terrain. A cover tile loses its terrain when no terrain sample is above water − 1.8 m
  (`kCoverWaterDepth`, `SoupOptions::cover_water`, keyed by a digest of the planes). Kharg: 1,290 of 1,614 cover
  tiles are under water. That took G4 Kharg from 11.3 s (cs 0.25, no rule) to 3.8–4.3 s.
* **Unwired Rush triggers** (open question 7): some Rush CombatAreaTrigger chains end at an interface field the
  instantiating scope never links. `read_mode_areas` counts them in `ModeAreas::unwired_triggers` and does not fail:
  Metro RL 2, SR 6; Caspian RL 6, SR 6. A chain that reaches a target of the wrong type is still an error.
* **The main component is chosen per mode by rows** (`bot_point_rows(..., p_main_from_rows)`): it is the SCC
  holding the most on-mesh G2 rows (ties: the mode's area-in-combat-polygons main, then area). The "largest area"
  rule failed on Metro TDM, where buried terrain under the city outweighed the station: 0/102 spawns before, 92/102
  after. `mode_main_component` (area inside the mode's combat areas) remains the tie-break and the fallback.
* **Reachability classes:** `nav_components_reaching` marks the components that can reach main. Failing rows are
  printed as exit-only (reaches main one way, e.g. a spawn on a ledge that drops in), entry-only, disconnected or
  off-mesh.
* **Drop links:** `drop_reach` is 1.5 m. Revision 3's 1.0 m put the falling capsule on the lip; sim.nav.unit
  covers this. Detour tile validation accepts detail boundary edges.
* **Nav-world cache:** `encode_world_index` wrote the tile count as u32, while `BlobReader::count` reads u64, so the
  warm path never hit. It is fixed: Metro CQL is cold 2.43 s and warm 0.16 s (whole nav world from the cache).
* **Godot** (§12) as designed, plus `--shot-delay=<s>`, a HUD suffix (others in the snapshot / total), and a log line
  with where the presented others stand when the shots start.

### 19.5 Recommendation (G2 failed; plan R6's fallback)

G1, G3 and G4 pass with wide margins:

* G1: the p99 is 5x under budget;
* G4: half the threshold;
* the bots' sources p99 is under the 2 ms budget.

There is no case for the R1 fallback (30 Hz, fewer sweeps, own BVH). The tail share is motor 66–77 % and sources
11–34 %, and the motor step is 0.5-1.0 ms mean for 64 soldiers. G2 fails for reasons the plan's R6 row anticipates.
Recommended, in order:

1. **Keep the nav exactly the motor's set**, as built (§5.1 proven). Do not open passages in the nav alone.
2. **Rush: bake per stage** (or per stage group), or treat stage-gated objects as Detour tile-cache obstacles.
   `ParkEntranceUndestroyed` is in the soup of every Rush stage today. M2b should read which objects the Rush stage
   logic hides or shows, and drop them from both the motor and the nav per stage.
3. **Judge G2 only on played points**: an objective outside every wired combat area of its mode, or with no
   geometry under it, is a parked crate (`in_combat_area` in sim_nav_install_tests.cpp is the first half). The
   Caspian SR sky crates need the second half: the stage areas' polygons or a "no ground within 50 m" rule.
4. **Dynamic props out of both sets**: `DynamicModelEntityData` "banger" props (cardboard boxes) are pushed or
   destroyed in BF3. Classify them in the soup (the tag flag plus `static_layer_of` route of §5.7 step 3), for the
   motor and the nav together. This needs the Physics data to confirm they are dynamic bodies.
5. **Jump/climb links** for 0.3–0.6 m steps and ~1 m platform edges. These match BF3's vault, which our motor lacks;
   they belong in M2b with the motor's vault.
6. **Spawn reader**: explain Caspian RL's 24 spawns 30 m under the terrain before judging them.

Until then, bots stay correct because of the spawn filter (§8): they spawn only in main and draw goals from their own
component, so G1 does not depend on G2.
